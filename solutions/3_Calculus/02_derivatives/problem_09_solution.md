## Question

For

$$
f(x)=\ln(x^2+1)
$$

compute the derivative. Then determine the sign of $f'(x)$ for $x<0$, $x=0$, and $x>0$, and describe qualitatively how the function behaves around zero.

## Solution

By the chain rule:

$$
f'(x) = \frac{2x}{x^2 + 1}
$$

Since $x^2 + 1 > 0$ for all $x \in \mathbb{R}$:
- For $x < 0$: $f'(x) < 0$ (decreasing).
- For $x = 0$: $f'(0) = 0$ (horizontal tangent).
- For $x > 0$: $f'(x) > 0$ (increasing).

Around $x = 0$, $f$ decreases to a local minimum of $f(0) = \ln 1 = 0$ at $x = 0$ and then increases for $x > 0$.
