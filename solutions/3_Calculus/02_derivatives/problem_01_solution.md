## Question

For

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4
$$

find $f'(x)$. Then compute $f'(0)$ and $f'(1)$, determine the signs of the obtained values, and explain what they say about the slope of the tangent line to the graph at these two points.

## Solution

Using the power and sum rules:

$$
f'(x) = 15x^4 - 6x^2 + 7
$$

$$
f'(0) = 15(0)^4 - 6(0)^2 + 7 = 7 \quad (>0)
$$

$$
f'(1) = 15(1)^4 - 6(1)^2 + 7 = 16 \quad (>0)
$$

Both $f'(0) > 0$ and $f'(1) > 0$, so the tangent lines at $x = 0$ and $x = 1$ have positive slopes and rise from left to right (with a steeper slope at $x = 1$).
