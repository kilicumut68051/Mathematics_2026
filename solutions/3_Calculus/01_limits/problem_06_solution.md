## Question

Compute

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2-x+1}{5x^2+4x-2}.
$$

Compare the method with Exercise 1. Explain what changes when moving from $n$ to a real variable $x$, and what stays the same.

## Solution

$$
\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3x^2-x+1}{5x^2+4x-2} = \lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{3 - \frac{1}{x} + \frac{1}{x^2}}{5 + \frac{4}{x} - \frac{2}{x^2}} = \frac{3}{5}
$$

The algebraic steps (dividing by the highest power $x^2$) remain the same as in Exercise 1; what changes is that $x$ varies continuously over $\mathbb{R}$ rather than taking discrete positive integer values $n$.
