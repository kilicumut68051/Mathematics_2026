## Question

Compute

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n.
$$

Calculate several values for increasing $n$ and compare the observed behavior with the value of the limit. Explain why a few values do not constitute a proof.

## Solution

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(1+\frac1n\right)^n = e \approx 2.71828
$$

- $n = 1$: $2$
- $n = 10$: $(1.1)^{10} \approx 2.5937$
- $n = 100$: $(1.01)^{100} \approx 2.7048$
- $n = 1000$: $(1.001)^{1000} \approx 2.7169$

A finite sample of terms only shows initial numerical behavior and cannot establish convergence or rule out different behavior for arbitrarily large $n$.
