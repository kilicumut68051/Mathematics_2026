## Question

Analyze

$$
f(x)=|x^2-4|.
$$

First sketch $x^2-4$, then explain the effect of the absolute value. Indicate the points where the derivative does not exist and justify the reason geometrically.

## Solution

- $g(x) = x^2 - 4$ is a parabola opening upward with vertex $(0, -4)$ and $x$-intercepts at $x = -2$ and $x = 2$.
- Taking the absolute value reflects the negative portion on $(-2, 2)$ across the $x$-axis upward, turning $(0, -4)$ into a local maximum at $(0, 4)$ and leaving $f(x) \ge 0$ everywhere.
- $f$ is decreasing on $(-\infty, -2)$ and $(0, 2)$, and increasing on $(-2, 0)$ and $(2, \infty)$.
- Non-differentiable points: $x = -2$ and $x = 2$, where $f'_-(-2) = -4 \neq f'_+(-2) = 4$ and $f'_-(2) = -4 \neq f'_+(2) = 4$. Geometrically, the reflection creates sharp corners (kinks) at $(-2, 0)$ and $(2, 0)$ with no unique tangent line.
