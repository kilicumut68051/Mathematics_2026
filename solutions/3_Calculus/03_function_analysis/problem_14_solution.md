## Question

It is known that

$$f'(x)=(x-1)(x+2).$$

Determine the monotonicity and the locations of the extrema, and sketch a possible qualitative shape of the function. Then, for

$$g(x)=\frac{x^3}{3}+\frac{x^2}{2}-2x$$

verify that $g'(x)=(x-1)(x+2)$, and compare the specific graph with the earlier qualitative sketch.

## Solution

From $f'(x) = (x - 1)(x + 2)$:
- On $(-\infty, -2)$: $f'(x) > 0 \implies$ increasing.
- On $(-2, 1)$: $f'(x) < 0 \implies$ decreasing.
- On $(1, \infty)$: $f'(x) > 0 \implies$ increasing.
- Local maximum at $x = -2$; local minimum at $x = 1$.

Differentiating $g(x)$:

$$g'(x) = x^2 + x - 2 = (x - 1)(x + 2)$$

$g(x)$ has the exact qualitative shape predicted above, with specific values $g(-2) = \frac{10}{3}$ (local maximum) and $g(1) = -\frac{7}{6}$ (local minimum), fixing the vertical shift ($g(0) = 0$) that was arbitrary in the qualitative sketch.
