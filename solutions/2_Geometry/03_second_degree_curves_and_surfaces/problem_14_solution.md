## Question

Compare

$$
x^2+y^2=z^2
$$

and

$$
x^2+y^2=4.
$$

For each surface, describe the cross-sections $z=c$, $x=0$, and $y=0$, make a sketch, and explain why the radius of the cross-section depends on $z$ in the first case but is constant in the second.

## Solution

$x^2 + y^2 = z^2$ (cone):
- $z = c$: circle $x^2 + y^2 = c^2$ of radius $|c|$
- $x = 0$: lines $y = \pm z$
- $y = 0$: lines $x = \pm z$

$x^2 + y^2 = 4$ (cylinder):
- $z = c$: circle $x^2 + y^2 = 4$ of radius $2$
- $x = 0$: lines $y = \pm 2$
- $y = 0$: lines $x = \pm 2$

In the cone, the radius is $|z|$ (depends linearly on $z$); in the cylinder, $z$ is absent from the equation, keeping the radius constant at $2$.
