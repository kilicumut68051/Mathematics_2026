## Question

Consider

$$
\frac{x^2}{9}+\frac{y^2}{4}+\frac{z^2}{16}=1.
$$

Give the semiaxis lengths, describe the cross-section $z=0$, and explain how the cross-sections $z=c$ change for $|c|<4$.

## Solution

Semi-axes: $a = 3$, $b = 2$, $c_z = 4$.
Cross-section $z = 0$: $\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$ (ellipse with semi-axes $3$ and $2$).
For $|c| < 4$: $\frac{x^2}{9(1 - c^2/16)} + \frac{y^2}{4(1 - c^2/16)} = 1$, ellipses whose semi-axes shrink smoothly as $|c|$ increases toward $4$, reducing to a point at $c = \pm 4$.
